## Overview

Mark Zuckerberg is the co-founder and CEO of [[Meta]], and in the sources collected here, dated 2023 to 2026, he appears mainly as the author of Meta's public case for calling its AI models open source. In June 2023, U.S. Senators Richard Blumenthal and Josh Hawley wrote to him about the March leak of Meta's LLaMA model. VentureBeat reported that he then reaffirmed Meta's "commitment to an open science-based approach to AI research" at a company all-hands meeting. MIT Technology Review in March 2024 and Euronews in October 2024 both quote an earnings-call remark of his, placed by Euronews in a February call, that open source software "often becomes an industry standard" and that companies standardizing on Meta's stack make new innovations easier to integrate into Meta's products.

In a July 2024 letter signed "MZ", he called Llama 3.1 405B "the first frontier-level open source AI model" and argued that [[OpenSourceAI]] is good for developers, good for Meta because "selling access to AI models isn't our business model", and good for the world. He wrote that open source AI "will be safer than the alternatives", that larger actors with more compute can check bad actors as long as everyone has access to similar generations of models, and that closing models off from China "will not work and will only disadvantage the US and its allies". He traced his support for open ecosystems to building services under Apple's platform rules. The letter is Meta's own account, with no third-party voice, and it applies the "open source" label without testing it against any definition.

## Limits and Reception

His statements also set limits on openness. TechCrunch reports that on a podcast in 2024 he said Meta would not open source a model if a qualitative change in capability made that irresponsible, and that his July 2025 "personal superintelligence" letter said Meta must be "careful about what we choose to open source"; a Meta spokesperson answered that its position was unchanged and that it expects to train a mix of open and closed models. In August 2026, VentureBeat reports, he wrote on X that Meta was opening the [[OpenWeights|weights]] of Muse Glimmer, released under Apache 2.0, and would soon release those of Muse Spark 1.2, adding that "Meta is a strong supporter of open source". VentureBeat's Carl Franzen notes that only the weights are open, since Meta has not released the [[TrainingData|training data]] or training code, and calls the Apache 2.0 grant more permissive than Llama's community license ever was.

Critics answer his use of the label. The [[OpenSourceInitiative]] (OSI), through [[StefanoMaffulli|Stefano Maffulli]] in Euronews, says Meta's terms of use and distribution fail open source principles. Euronews reports that Meta did not reply to its request for comment. A February 2025 OSI post calls on the community to ask Zuckerberg and Yann LeCun to bring the Llama license into line with the [[OpenSourceDefinition]], naming Meta's use of the term [[OpenWashing]]. The Register's Steven J. Vaughan-Nichols reports that Zuckerberg describes Llama 3 as open source and argues its license fails that definition. Bradley M. Kühn of the Software Freedom Conservancy, faulting the OSI rather than Meta, argues that the OSI, lacking a trademark, cannot stop him applying the label to Llama. Luis Villa told MIT Technology Review that "Mark Zuckerberg is going to tell us all what he thinks 'open' means, and he has a very big megaphone." TIME quotes Andrea Miotti of Control AI and Hamza Tariq Chaudhry of the Future of Life Institute rejecting the letter's [[AISafety]] and China arguments.
