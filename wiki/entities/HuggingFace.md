## Overview

Hugging Face appears in this wiki's sources mainly as a venue where openly released models are distributed. [[Meta]] made Llama 2 available through Amazon Web Services, Hugging Face, and other providers, and Ai2 released OLMo 7B on Hugging Face and GitHub on 2024-02-01. On 2025-08-05 a Hugging Face blog post welcomed OpenAI's GPT OSS family as "a hugely anticipated [[OpenWeights|open-weights]] release." The post headlined the family as "open-source," but it described only the released weights. Most of the post covered integration with Hugging Face tooling, including its Inference Providers service and the trl library for [[FineTuning|fine-tuning]].

Hugging Face staff and the company have also signed pro-openness statements. The Joint Statement on AI Safety and Openness, hosted on a [[Mozilla]] site, lists Julien Chaumond and Yacine Jernite with Hugging Face among its signatories. The 2026-07-24 open letter "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" includes Hugging Face in its published signatory list.

## Key Facts

- **Defensive case for open weights** — TechCrunch reports that Hugging Face relied on Z.ai's open-weight GLM-5.2 to defend itself against an AI-powered cyberattack that the article calls "OpenAI's breach" and does not describe further. Hugging Face CEO Clem Delangue argues that such systems can help defend against "millions of cyberattacks every day." SaferAI's Henry Papadatos, quoted in the same article, calls this defensive benefit often overstated.
- **Named by a critic** — David Evan Harris names Hugging Face among the companies releasing "unsecured" AI systems. He also states that "Llama 2 Uncensored," a Llama 2 derivative with its safety features stripped away, is hosted for free download on the Hugging Face repository.
- **Reader dispute over the "open" label** — commenters on the GPT OSS post dispute calling the release open, since it does not share the code needed to train the model. The post itself offers no independent assessment of the release's openness.
