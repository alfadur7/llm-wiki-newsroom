## Overview

The Linux Foundation, whose CEO is Jim Zemlin, appears in these sources as the organization behind two AI-openness instruments — the [[OpenMDW]] model license and the tiered [[ModelOpennessFramework]]. It announced on 2026-05-28 the release of OpenMDW-1.1, a permissive license framework written for AI model distributions, and said that NVIDIA plans to adopt it for its Cosmos, Isaac GR00T, Ising and Nemotron model families. According to its own announcement, the Linux Foundation launched OpenMDW in 2025 with the PyTorch Foundation, and Zemlin said adoption gives organizations "greater confidence to build, contribute, and deploy AI systems in the open."

The Linux Foundation's Mike Dolan submitted OpenMDW to the [[OpenSourceInitiative|Open Source Initiative]] (OSI) for approval. LWN's Jonathan Corbet reports that reviewers on the OSI list objected to its rights-clearing disclaimer and its termination clause, and that the OSI did not appear ready to approve it in its current form. Dolan defended the termination clause as giving the license symmetry, and former OSI executive director [[StefanoMaffulli|Stefano Maffulli]], in The Register, said the review appears "tainted by an ideological bias". The Register's Steven J. Vaughan-Nichols calls the approach reasonable and ends without a verdict on adoption.

The Model Openness Framework grades models in three tiers. Open Future's Alek Tarkowski and Paul Keller argue that such a tiered standard would have resolved the OSI definition's training-data question most easily, and the University of Geneva's Yaniv Benhamou and Michel Reymond set it against the binary test of the OSI's Open Source AI Definition (OSAID). Stanford HAI's James Landay says Stanford HAI aligns its bar for [[OpenSourceAI]] with the framework's top "Open Science" class. InfoWorld lists the Linux Foundation among the more than 25 organizations in the OSAID's co-design process, and TechCrunch reports that [[Meta]] pointed to the Linux Foundation's suggested definitions as another effort to codify open source AI.

## Key Facts

- **EU AI Act guide** — Linux Foundation researchers co-wrote, with [[HuggingFace]] and [[Mozilla]] researchers, a 2025-08-04 guide to the [[EUAIAct]]'s rules for general-purpose AI models; the authors state it gives their personal views, not their organizations' positions, and judge that OpenMDW likely qualifies as a free and open-source license under the Act.
- **Open weights letter** — the Linux Foundation appears in the published signatory list of a 2026-07-24 open letter, hosted by Microsoft, urging US policymakers not to prohibit [[OpenWeights]].
