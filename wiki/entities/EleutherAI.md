## Overview

EleutherAI, whose releases include the GPT-NeoX model and The Pile and Common Pile training datasets, released the Common Pile v0.1 in June 2025 (TechCrunch, June 6, 2025), an 8-terabyte [[TrainingData|training dataset]] of openly licensed and public-domain text that TechCrunch reports was built over about two years with Poolside, [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]], other startups and academic institutions ([[eleutherai-common-pile-dataset]]). EleutherAI says two 7-billion-parameter models trained on part of it, Comma v0.1-1T and Comma v0.1-2T, rival [[Meta]]'s first Llama model on coding, image understanding and math benchmarks; these are its own claims, with no external assessment in the source. Executive director Stella Biderman clarified that EleutherAI contributed to the release alongside many partners, including the University of Toronto, which helped lead the research. TechCrunch reads the dataset as partly an effort to right EleutherAI's earlier release of The Pile, which included copyrighted material. Biderman argues that the common idea that unlicensed text drives performance is "unjustified."

In policy debates EleutherAI and its researchers have argued for open release. In 2023 Biderman, then described as an AI researcher at Booz Allen Hamilton and EleutherAI, called open-source research essential for studying models outside the companies with a financial interest in them ([[open-source-ai-llm-access-debate]]). With [[Mozilla]] and Hugging Face, EleutherAI sent an August 8, 2024 letter on California's SB 1047 asking legislators to broaden the bill's definition of an [[OpenSourceAI|open-source AI]] model ([[mozilla-eleutherai-hf-sb-1047-letter]]), and it is listed among the more than 270 signatories of the July 24, 2026 open letter defending [[OpenWeights|open weight]] models ([[open-weights-american-ai-leadership]]).

## Key Facts

- **Models cited by others** — JJ Jasser names EleutherAI's GPT-NeoX among the few models that meet his four criteria of public architecture, training code, weights and training data, all under unrestricted licenses, in an essay on [[OpenWashing|open-washing]] ([[open-washing-four-criteria]]); Christopher Manning puts GPT-NeoX (GPT-Neo-X in his wording) at 20 billion parameters ([[senators-question-meta-llama-leak]]).
- **Harris's criticism** — David Evan Harris lists EleutherAI among the companies releasing what he calls "unsecured" AI systems, which he argues pose a risk to [[AISafety|AI safety]] that cannot yet be contained ([[open-source-ai-uniquely-dangerous]]).
