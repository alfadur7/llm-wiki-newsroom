## Overview

The Model Openness Framework is the Linux Foundation's scheme for grading machine learning models in three tiers. Alek Tarkowski and Paul Keller of Open Future describe the tiers as measuring completeness and openness. Yaniv Benhamou and Michel Reymond describe them as grading the openness of AI systems. TechCrunch's Paul Sawers reports that the framework comes from an academic paper that rates models on completeness and openness. He also reports that the checklist of required components in the [[OpenSourceInitiative]]'s draft Open Source AI Definition (OSAID) was based on that paper.

In this wiki's sources the framework is mostly cited as the graded alternative to the OSAID's binary test for [[OpenSourceAI]]. The Open Source Initiative chose a binary, non-tiered standard, arguing that it must be clear which systems comply. Tarkowski and Keller argue that a tiered standard like the Model Openness Framework would have resolved the [[TrainingData]] question most easily. Benhamou and Reymond call the OSAID a "take it or leave it" approach and argue that a tiered notion of openness, as in the Model Openness Framework, may be preferable.

## Key Points

- **Tier count** — both 2024–2025 commentaries report three tiers; neither commentary names or describes the individual tiers.
- **Other graded schemes** — Tarkowski and Keller list the framework next to [[Mozilla]]'s Framework for Openness in Foundation Models and the Digital Public Goods Alliance's recommendations on AI as a digital public good.
