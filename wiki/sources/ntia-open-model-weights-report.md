## Summary

A press release from the U.S. Department of Commerce's National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) announces its Report on Dual-Use Foundation Models with Widely Available Model Weights. According to NTIA, the report recommends that the U.S. government refrain from immediately restricting the wide availability of [[OpenWeights|open model weights]] in the largest AI systems, while building new capabilities to monitor their risks. The monitoring program it calls for would collect and evaluate evidence of risks and benefits and act on it, including possible restrictions on weight availability if warranted. The release quotes Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo and NTIA Administrator Alan Davidson; it carries no explicit date, though its URL places it in 2024, and external assessment of the report is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] NTIA — issued policy recommendations that embrace openness in AI while calling for active monitoring of risks in powerful AI models
- [fact] NTIA — recommends in its Report on Dual-Use Foundation Models with Widely Available Model Weights that the U.S. government refrain from immediately restricting the wide availability of [[OpenWeights|open model weights]] in the largest AI systems
- [fact] NTIA — recommends that the U.S. government develop new capabilities to monitor for potential risks from open-weight models
- [fact] NTIA — calls for an ongoing government program to collect evidence of the risks and benefits of widely available model weights, evaluate that evidence, and act on those evaluations
- [fact] NTIA — lists possible restrictions on model weight availability, if warranted, among the actions the monitoring program could take
- [fact] NTIA — states that [[OpenWeights|open-weight]] models let developers build upon and adapt previous work, broadening AI tools' availability to small companies, researchers, nonprofits, and individuals
- [fact] NTIA — states that the President's Executive Order on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence directed it to review the risks and benefits of large AI models with widely available weights and to develop policy recommendations
- [forecast] NTIA — expects its recommendations to promote innovation and access while positioning the U.S. government to respond quickly to risks that may arise from future models
- [fact] Gina Raimondo — said, as U.S. Secretary of Commerce, that the report provides a roadmap for responsible AI innovation and American leadership by embracing openness [[ntia-open-model-weights-report#Key Quotes]]
- [fact] Alan Davidson — said, as NTIA Administrator, that the openness of the largest AI systems will affect competition, innovation, and risks [[ntia-open-model-weights-report#Key Quotes]]
- [fact] Alan Davidson — said that government has a key role in supporting AI development while building capacity to understand and address new risks [[ntia-open-model-weights-report#Key Quotes]]

## Key Quotes

> "The Biden-Harris Administration is pulling every lever to maximize the promise of AI while minimizing its risks. Today's report provides a roadmap for responsible AI innovation and American leadership by embracing openness and recommending how the U.S. government can prepare for and adapt to potential challenges ahead." — Gina Raimondo, U.S. Secretary of Commerce

> "The openness of the largest and most powerful AI systems will affect competition, innovation and risks in these revolutionary tools." — Alan Davidson, Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Communications and Information and NTIA Administrator

> "NTIA's report recognizes the importance of open AI systems and calls for more active monitoring of risks from the wide availability of model weights for the largest AI models. Government has a key role to play in supporting AI development while building capacity to understand and address new risks." — Alan Davidson, Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Communications and Information and NTIA Administrator
