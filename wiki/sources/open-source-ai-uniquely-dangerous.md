## Summary

In an IEEE Spectrum guest post, David Evan Harris, a former [[Meta]] employee, argues that "unsecured" AI systems, which he says are sometimes called open source, pose a risk society cannot yet contain. He points to [[Meta]]'s Llama 2 and its derivative "Llama 2 Uncensored," which had its safety features stripped away, and he warns of misuse in disinformation, scams, deepfake pornography, and chemical or biological weapons. He does not call for rules aimed only at unsecured AI. Instead he proposes rules for all AI systems, starting with a pause on new unsecured releases, plus rules for distribution channels and new government bodies. The piece is one half of a paired debate; the opposing post, "Open Source AI Is Good for Us," is not in the raw.

## Key Claims

- [analysis] David Evan Harris — describes closed-source systems such as ChatGPT as ones whose makers keep the software secure while selling access through a web interface or an API
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — says the public understands too little about the rapid and uncontrolled release of powerful unsecured AI systems, which he notes are sometimes called open source
- [fact] David Evan Harris — states that OpenAI's leaders decided in 2019 that releasing its GPT systems' source code and model weights was too dangerous to continue
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — says [[Meta]] moved in the opposite direction, releasing powerful unsecured AI systems in the name of democratizing access
- [fact] David Evan Harris — names Stability AI, Hugging Face, Mistral, EleutherAI, and the Technology Innovation Institute as other companies releasing unsecured AI systems
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — says these companies and allied advocacy groups made limited progress in winning exemptions for some unsecured models in the EU AI Act
- [forecast] David Evan Harris — expects these groups may seek similar exemptions in the United States through the public comment period set by the White House AI Executive Order
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — supports a role for the open-source movement in AI so that no single entity acts as a gatekeeper to the technology
- [fact] David Evan Harris — states that [[Meta]] released Llama 2 with a 27-page "Responsible Use Guide"
- [fact] David Evan Harris — states that "Llama 2 Uncensored," a derivative of Llama 2 with its safety features stripped away, is hosted for free download on the Hugging Face repository
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — argues that once an "uncensored" version of an unsecured system is released, the original maker is largely powerless to act
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — argues that jailbreaks of secured systems can be fixed as they are discovered, unlike the misuse of unsecured systems
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — warns that sophisticated threat actors can download unsecured models, disable their safety features, and build custom versions for abuse
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — says social media and messaging platforms cannot yet detect AI-generated content at scale, which leaves them open to personalized misinformation and scams
- [forecast] David Evan Harris — warns that this misuse could have catastrophic effects on the information ecosystem and on elections in particular
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — says unsecured AI could help produce biological and chemical weapons, a chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear (CBRN) risk the White House Executive Order also names
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — argues that nearly all publicly discussed AI regulations apply to secured systems too, and that secured developers comply more easily because they can monitor abuse and ship updates
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes pausing all new releases of unsecured AI systems until developers meet his requirements in ways that stop bad actors from easily removing safety features
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes registration and licensing, both retroactive and ongoing, of all AI systems above a capability threshold
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes making developers legally liable for "reasonably foreseeable misuse" and negligence
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes requiring training-data transparency and banning training on personally identifiable information and on content related to biological and chemical weapons
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes "know your customer" procedures for sales of powerful AI hardware and cloud services, similar to those used by financial institutions
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes mandatory disclosure of AI vulnerabilities and failures to a designated government authority
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes requiring social media companies to implement the C2PA Content Credentials labeling standard by a deadline
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes a nimble regulatory body with the authority to approve or reject risk assessments and to block model deployment
- [fact] David Evan Harris — states that 28 countries signed the Bletchley Declaration, including the United States, China, the United Kingdom, the United Arab Emirates, France, and Germany
- [analysis] David Evan Harris — proposes democratizing AI access through public AI infrastructure with powerful secured models rather than through unsecured systems
- [fact] Gary Marcus — told Harris by email that how open-source AI is regulated is the most important unresolved issue in the immediate term
- [forecast] David Evan Harris — expects that industry will not take these actions unless forced, and that companies producing unsecured AI may earn billions while pushing the risks onto everyone else

## Key Quotes

> "As things stand today, unsecured AI poses an enormous risk that we are not yet able to contain." — David Evan Harris, former Meta employee

> "Once someone releases an 'uncensored' version of an unsecured AI system, the original maker of the system is largely powerless to do anything about it." — David Evan Harris, former Meta employee

> "We don't need to specifically regulate unsecured AI—nearly all of the regulations that have been publicly discussed apply to secured AI systems as well." — David Evan Harris, former Meta employee

> "I think how we regulate open-source AI is THE most important unresolved issue in the immediate term." — Gary Marcus, cognitive scientist and professor emeritus at New York University

> "If actions like these are not taken, companies producing unsecured AI may bring in billions of dollars in profits while pushing the risks posed by their products onto all of us." — David Evan Harris, former Meta employee
