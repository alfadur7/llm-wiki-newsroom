## Summary

In a letter on [[Meta]]'s newsroom, signed "MZ," Mark Zuckerberg announces the release of Llama 3.1 405B, which he calls "the first frontier-level open source AI model," alongside updated 70B and 8B models. He argues that [[OpenSourceAI]] is good for developers, who can fine-tune and distill models on their own data without vendor lock-in; good for Meta, whose business model does not depend on selling model access; and good for the world, because he expects open models to prove safer than closed ones and to keep power from concentrating in a few companies. He compares Llama's path to that of Linux against closed Unix. It is a vendor letter with no third-party voice, and it applies the "open source" label to Llama without weighing it against any definition — external assessment is outside the raw's scope. The raw carries no publication date; the URL path places it in July 2024.

## Key Claims

- [fact] [[Meta]] — released Llama 3.1 405B, which it calls the first frontier-level open source AI model, along with new Llama 3.1 70B and 8B models
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues AI will follow the path of open source Linux, which overtook the closed Unix variants to become the industry-standard foundation for cloud computing and most mobile operating systems
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — says Llama 2 was comparable only to an older generation of models, while Llama 3 is competitive with the most advanced models and leads in some areas
- [forecast] Mark Zuckerberg — expects future Llama models to become the most advanced in the industry starting the year after the letter
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — claims Llama already leads on openness, modifiability, and cost efficiency
- [forecast] Mark Zuckerberg — expects the open 405B model to be the best choice for fine-tuning and distilling smaller models
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — states that Amazon, Databricks, and NVIDIA are launching service suites for developers to fine-tune and distill their own Llama models
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — states that the models will be available on all major clouds, including AWS, Azure, Google, and Oracle
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — states that Groq has built low-latency, low-cost inference serving for the new models
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues open models let organizations train and fine-tune on their own data and distill a model to their optimal size without Meta or anyone else seeing that data
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues open source keeps organizations from being locked into a closed vendor that can change its model, alter its terms, or stop serving them
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues open models protect sensitive data because organizations can run them wherever they want instead of sending data to closed models over cloud APIs
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — states that developers can run inference on Llama 3.1 405B on their own infrastructure at roughly 50% of the cost of closed models like GPT-4o
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — cites building services under Apple's platform rules as a formative experience behind his support for open ecosystems in AI and AR/VR
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues open sourcing Llama gives away no massive advantage, because AI development will stay competitive
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues that because selling access to AI models is not Meta's business model, releasing Llama openly does not undercut Meta's revenue
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — says several closed model providers consistently lobby governments against open source
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — states that Meta saved billions of dollars by releasing its server, network, and data center designs through the Open Compute Project
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — names PyTorch and React as leading tools Meta open sourced and whose ecosystems it benefited from
- [forecast] Mark Zuckerberg — expects open source AI to be safer than the alternatives and expects governments to conclude that supporting it is in their interest
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — divides AI harm into unintentional harm, which he says covers most concerns, and intentional harm by bad actors
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues open source is significantly safer against unintentional harm because open systems are more transparent and can be widely scrutinized
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — proposes that the baseline for judging a model's harm be whether it facilitates more harm than information already retrievable from Google or other search results
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues that wide AI deployment lets larger actors with more compute check smaller bad actors, as long as everyone has access to similar generations of models
- [analysis] Mark Zuckerberg — argues that closing models to keep them from China will not work, because adversaries are good at espionage and models that fit on a thumb drive are easy to steal
- [forecast] Mark Zuckerberg — expects a world of only closed models to leave leading models with a few big companies and geopolitical adversaries while startups, universities, and small businesses miss out
- [fact] Mark Zuckerberg — states that Meta is building internal teams and partnerships to help developers and partners use Llama, unlike its approach with past Llama models
- [forecast] Mark Zuckerberg — expects the Llama 3.1 release to be an inflection point at which most developers begin to primarily use open source

## Key Quotes

> "Today we’re taking the next steps towards open source AI becoming the industry standard. We’re releasing Llama 3.1 405B, the first frontier-level open source AI model, as well as new and improved Llama 3.1 70B and 8B models." — Mark Zuckerberg, author of the letter

> "Developers can run inference on Llama 3.1 405B on their own infra at roughly 50% the cost of using closed models like GPT-4o, for both user-facing and offline inference tasks." — Mark Zuckerberg, author of the letter

> "Third, a key difference between Meta and closed model providers is that selling access to AI models isn’t our business model. That means openly releasing Llama doesn’t undercut our revenue, sustainability, or ability to invest in research like it does for closed providers." — Mark Zuckerberg, author of the letter

> "There is an ongoing debate about the safety of open source AI models, and my view is that open source AI will be safer than the alternatives." — Mark Zuckerberg, author of the letter

> "Some people argue that we must close our models to prevent China from gaining access to them, but my view is that this will not work and will only disadvantage the US and its allies." — Mark Zuckerberg, author of the letter

> "The bottom line is that open source AI represents the world’s best shot at harnessing this technology to create the greatest economic opportunity and security for everyone." — Mark Zuckerberg, author of the letter
