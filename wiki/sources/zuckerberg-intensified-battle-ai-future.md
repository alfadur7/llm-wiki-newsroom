## Summary

A TIME news analysis reports that [[Meta]] CEO [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] released a new generation of Llama models alongside a manifesto for [[OpenSourceAI|open-source AI]], and reads the letter as a political move against pending AI regulation. It contrasts Meta's release of [[OpenWeights|open weights]] with [[OpenAI]], Google DeepMind and [[Anthropic]], which sell model access through browsers and APIs, and it summarizes the letter's arguments on the Apple precedent, [[AISafety|safety]] and China. Two AI-safety voices answer the letter: Andrea Miotti of Control AI and Hamza Tariq Chaudhry of the Future of Life Institute. Meta and Zuckerberg speak only through the quoted letter, with no fresh comment in the piece; the raw carries no author or publication date and dates the release only as "Tuesday."

## Key Claims

- [fact] [[Meta]] — released its latest generation of Llama large language models and claims the biggest one is the first open-source model to reach the frontier of AI capabilities
- [analysis] TIME — reports that OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Anthropic sell access to their AIs through web browsers or APIs, which lets them protect intellectual property, monitor use and bar bad actors
- [analysis] TIME — reports that Meta instead releases the weights of its Llama models so that anybody can download and run them on their own machines
- [analysis] TIME — reports that Meta's open-weights strategy has put its competitors under financial pressure
- [analysis] TIME — reports that many in AI safety warn that open-sourcing powerful models has already led to harms such as deepfakes and could lead to worse dangers
- [analysis] TIME — calls the manifesto a deft political move, since recent polling suggests the American public would welcome laws restricting potentially dangerous AI
- [analysis] TIME — reports that California's SB1047 and the ENFORCE Act in Washington, D.C., would limit the kinds of systems companies like Meta can open-source
- [analysis] TIME — reports that many venture capitalists and tech CEOs who praised the letter have mounted a campaign against regulations that would constrain open-source AI releases
- [fact] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — writes that open source will spread AI's benefits more widely and keep power from concentrating in a small number of companies
- [analysis] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — traces his commitment to open source to Meta's struggle against Apple, whose iPhone platform constrains what Meta builds and whose App Store takes a cut of its revenue
- [analysis] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — suggests closed AI providers' safety concerns and lobbying against open source may stem from a business model built on selling access
- [analysis] TIME — reports critics' point that Llama models still carry usage restrictions that fall short of true open-source principles
- [fact] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — writes that open-source software has historically been more secure because transparent systems are more open to scrutiny
- [fact] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — writes that governments and institutions with more compute can check bad actors with less compute, as long as everyone has access to similar generations of models
- [fact] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — writes that closing off models from China will not work and will only disadvantage the U.S. and its allies
- [analysis] Andrea Miotti — calls the letter part of a trend of Silicon Valley CEOs and venture capitalists refusing to take responsibility for damages their AI may cause
- [analysis] Hamza Tariq Chaudhry — argues that not all large actors are benevolent and that authoritarian states will likely repurpose models like Llama
- [analysis] TIME — states that the letter does not address the offense-defense asymmetry, the risk that AI strengthens attackers more than defenders
- [analysis] Andrea Miotti — argues that against catastrophic dangers, offense needs to get lucky once while defense needs to get lucky every time

## Key Quotes

> "Open-source will ensure that more people around the world have access to the benefits and opportunities of AI, that power isn't concentrated in the hands of a small number of companies, and that the technology can be deployed more evenly and safely across society. It will make the world more prosperous and safer." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO

> "As long as everyone has access to similar generations of models—which open-source promotes—then governments and institutions with more compute resources will be able to check bad actors with less compute." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO

> "It seems most likely that a world of only closed models results in a small number of big companies plus our geopolitical adversaries having access to leading models, while startups, universities, and small businesses miss out on opportunities." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO

> "This letter is part of a broader trend of some Silicon Valley CEOs and venture capitalists refusing to take responsibility for damages their AI technology may cause. Including catastrophic outcomes." — Andrea Miotti, executive director of Control AI

> "The most authoritarian states will likely repurpose models like Llama to perpetuate their power and commit injustices." — Hamza Tariq Chaudhry, U.S. policy specialist at the Future of Life Institute

> "Coming from the Global South, I am acutely aware that AI-powered cyberattacks, disinformation campaigns and other harms pose a much greater danger to countries with nascent institutions and severe resource constraints, far away from Silicon Valley." — Hamza Tariq Chaudhry, U.S. policy specialist at the Future of Life Institute

> "When dealing with catastrophic dangers, it's a simple fact that offense needs only to get lucky once, but defense needs to get lucky every time. A virus can spread and kill in days, while deploying a treatment can take years." — Andrea Miotti, executive director of Control AI

> "Zuckerberg admits that advanced AI technology is easily stolen by hostile actors, but his solution is to just give it to them for free." — Andrea Miotti, executive director of Control AI
