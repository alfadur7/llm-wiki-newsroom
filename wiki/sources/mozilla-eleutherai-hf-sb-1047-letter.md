## Summary

A post on [[Mozilla]]'s Open Policy & Advocacy blog publishes the letter that Mozilla, [[EleutherAI]] and [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]] sent on August 8, 2024 to California State Senator Scott Wiener, Assembly members and Governor Gavin Newsom's office about SB 1047, the Safe and Secure Innovation for Frontier Artificial Intelligence Models Act. The three organizations welcome the bill's CalCompute proposal but say its vague definitions, developer obligations and compute thresholds would harm the open-source community. They ask the legislature to broaden the bill's definition of an open-source AI model, suggesting work with a body such as the [[OpenSourceInitiative|Open Source Initiative]]. An update dated August 30, 2024 notes the bill passed the legislature on August 29 and was heading to the Governor. The post states only the signatories' position; no response from Senator Wiener's office appears in the raw.

## Key Claims

- [fact] [[Mozilla]] — states that Mozilla, [[HuggingFace]] and EleutherAI sent a letter on SB 1047 to Senator Scott Wiener, members of the California Assembly and the Office of Governor Newsom on August 8, 2024
- [fact] [[Mozilla]] — reports that SB 1047 passed the California legislature on August 29, 2024 and was heading to the Governor's desk
- [fact] [[Mozilla]] — states that Senator Wiener introduced SB 1047 in early 2024 to address critical, and so far theoretical, harms from large AI models
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — describes SB 1047 as the target of intense criticism, mainly over its potential harm to the open-source community and users of open-source AI models
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — names Y Combinator founders and AI researcher Andrew Ng among those who publicly raised concerns about the bill
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — says its research and historical evidence point to open source as one of the clearest pathways to mitigating risk and bias and creating trustworthy AI
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — argues that open models lower the barriers for startups, small businesses, academic institutions and researchers to use AI
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — argues that open models help protect civil and human rights because researchers, civil society and regulators can more easily test their capabilities, risks and legal compliance
- [fact] [[Mozilla]] — writes in the joint letter that the signatories applaud the bill's proposal to create CalCompute to provide access to computational resources for building AI
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — writes in the joint letter that parts of the bill as written and amended will directly harm research, academic and small business communities that depend on open-source technology
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — asks in the joint letter that the bill's "reasonable assurance" standard be defined in consultation with the open-source, academic and business communities, calling the current wording open-ended
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — argues in the joint letter that general-purpose tools such as open-source AI models cannot control their end users' actions without serious harm to rights such as privacy
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — objects in the joint letter to the requirement that developers identify tests sufficient to provide reasonable assurance against critical harm, especially as it applies to covered model derivatives
- [fact] [[Mozilla]] — states in the joint letter that the bill gives the Frontier Model Division broad latitude after January 1, 2027 to decide which AI models are covered
- [forecast] [[Mozilla]] — expects in the joint letter that startups, researchers and academic institutions will soon surpass the bill's current compute threshold, making it obsolete
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — asks in the joint letter for statutory requirements that the Frontier Model Division regularly update the covered-model criteria in consultation with academia, civil society, the open-source community and businesses
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — argues in the joint letter that the bill's definition of an open-source AI model misses how researchers and businesses release openly available models, often with some legal or technical limitations
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — urges in the joint letter that the definition be broadened, possibly with a body such as the [[OpenSourceInitiative]], into a legal definition covering the spectrum of openly available AI
- [fact] [[Mozilla]] — reports that in early 2024 it and the Columbia Institute of Global Politics convened over 40 scholars and practitioners working on openness and AI
- [analysis] [[Mozilla]] — concludes in the joint letter that the bill needs significant changes to its fundamental structure to avoid significant harm to the open-source community

## Key Quotes

> "We hope that as Governor Newsom considers the merits of the legislation he considers the serious harms that this bill may do to the open-source ecosystem." — [[Mozilla]]

> "As you noted in your open letter, 'For decades, open sourcing has been a critical driver of innovation and security in the software world,'" — [[Mozilla]], [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]] and [[EleutherAI]], joint letter to Senator Scott Wiener

> "The developer of an everyday computer program like a word processor cannot reasonably provide assurance that someone will not use their program to draft a ransom note that is then used in a crime" — [[Mozilla]], [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]] and [[EleutherAI]], joint letter

> "Openness in AI has the potential to advance key societal goals, including making AI safe and effective, unlocking innovation and competition in the AI market, and bring underserved communities into the AI ecosystem." — [[Mozilla]] and Columbia Institute of Global Politics convening finding, as quoted in the joint letter

> "We are strong proponents of effective AI regulation, but we believe that AI risk management and regulatory requirements should be proportionally distributed across the development process based on factors such as technical feasibility and end user impact." — [[Mozilla]], [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]] and [[EleutherAI]], joint letter
