## Summary

A Stanford HAI policy brief, written almost one year after the "DeepSeek moment," argues that Chinese [[OpenWeights|open-weight]] language models have caught up with or pulled ahead of their global counterparts in capability and adoption. It profiles four Chinese open-weight model families and describes an ecosystem of many actors building computationally efficient models for flexible downstream deployment, with Chinese government support playing a substantial but not decisive role. The introduction traces the path from the 2022 U.S. chip export controls and [[OpenAI]]'s ChatGPT launch, through early Chinese models built on [[Meta]]'s Llama, to [[DeepSeek]]'s R1 release and open-weight releases by Alibaba and Baidu. The clipped page holds only the brief's executive summary and introduction; the model profiles, the policy analysis, the authors and the publication date are not in the raw.

## Key Claims

- [analysis] Stanford HAI — argues that Chinese AI models, especially open-weight LLMs, seem to have caught up with or pulled ahead of their global counterparts in advanced capabilities and adoption after years of lagging behind
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — finds that China's open-weight LLM ecosystem is driven by a wide range of actors prioritizing computationally efficient models optimized for flexible downstream deployment
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — observes that diverse commercial strategies for turning open-weight model adoption into business success are emerging, but that their long-term viability remains uncertain
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — assesses that Chinese government support for open-weight model development has played a substantial role in its success without being the sole determinant
- [forecast] Stanford HAI — states there is no guarantee that Chinese government support for open-weight model development will continue
- [forecast] Stanford HAI — expects widespread global adoption of Chinese open-weight models to potentially reshape technology access and reliance patterns and to affect AI governance, [[AISafety|safety]] and competition
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — recommends that policymakers ground their policy actions in a granular understanding of real-world deployment
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that in October 2022 the U.S. government imposed unilateral export controls on semiconductor manufacturing equipment and on the most powerful chips for LLM training
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that [[OpenAI]] launched ChatGPT the month after the October 2022 export controls
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that many Chinese labs initially built their models on top of [[Meta]]'s open-weight Llama model and its architecture
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that Hangzhou-based [[DeepSeek]] released its R1 reasoning model a little more than two years after ChatGPT's launch
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that the R1 release caused Nvidia the biggest single-day loss of any company in U.S. stock market history
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — says the broader U.S. discourse did not see DeepSeek's success coming, although some analysts had monitored its early development
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that Alibaba's Qwen models were in development long before DeepSeek's breakout and are widely used by developers around the world
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that Baidu, which had pursued a closed-model strategy, has turned to releasing the weights of some of its flagship models
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — says Chinese-made open-weight models are now unavoidable in the global competitive AI landscape
- [fact] Stanford HAI — notes that policymakers are weighing measures to restrict who can build at the AI cutting edge while Chinese models are being adopted around the world, including in the United States
