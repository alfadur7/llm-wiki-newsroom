## Summary

[[Meta]]'s newsroom announcement of Llama 2, which Meta describes as "the next generation of our open source large language model," released free of charge for research and commercial use with model weights and starting code for pretrained and fine-tuned versions. Meta names Microsoft its preferred partner, putting Llama 2 in the Azure AI model catalog and optimizing it for Windows, and argues that an open approach both spreads economic opportunity and makes AI safer. The post also lists Meta's responsibility measures, among them an Acceptable Use Policy that prohibits certain use cases; it does not reproduce the license terms themselves. It is a vendor announcement with no third-party voice — external assessment of its "open source" label is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] [[Meta]] — released Llama 2 free of charge for research and commercial use, calling the release open source
- [fact] [[Meta]] — included model weights and starting code for both the pretrained model and the conversational fine-tuned versions
- [fact] [[Meta]] — reported more than 100,000 requests from researchers for access to Llama 1
- [fact] [[Meta]] — named Microsoft its preferred partner for Llama 2, a partnership Satya Nadella announced on stage at Microsoft Inspire
- [fact] [[Meta]] — made Llama 2 available in the Azure AI model catalog starting on the announcement day
- [fact] [[Meta]] — optimized Llama 2 to run locally on Windows
- [fact] [[Meta]] — made Llama 2 available through Amazon Web Services, Hugging Face, and other providers
- [analysis] [[Meta]] — argues an open approach is right for generative AI because it gives businesses, startups, and researchers tools and computing power they could not build themselves
- [analysis] [[Meta]] — argues opening access to AI models is safer, because developers and researchers can stress test them and fix problems as a community
- [fact] [[Meta]] — red-teamed its fine-tuned models through internal efforts and commissioned third parties to run external adversarial testing
- [forecast] [[Meta]] — plans to release updated fine-tuned models based on continued safety fine-tuning and benchmarking
- [fact] [[Meta]] — disclosed known challenges and mitigations in a transparency schematic inside the Llama 2 research paper
- [fact] [[Meta]] — published a Responsible Use Guide of best practices for responsible development and safety evaluation
- [fact] [[Meta]] — put in place an Acceptable Use Policy that prohibits certain use cases of Llama 2
- [fact] [[Meta]] — launched the Open Innovation AI Research Community, a partnership program for academic researchers
- [fact] [[Meta]] — announced the Llama Impact Challenge for public, non-profit, and for-profit entities to apply Llama 2 to environmental, education, and other challenges
- [analysis] [[Meta]] — cites React and PyTorch, frameworks its engineers developed and shared, as open-source precedents that became industry standards
