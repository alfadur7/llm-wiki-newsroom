## Summary

The European Commission announces guidelines clarifying the scope of the AI Act's obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models, which enter into application on 2 August 2025. The guidelines define a general-purpose AI model by a training-compute threshold of 10^23 floating point operations, say when an actor modifying a model becomes a provider, set conditions under which models released under a free and open-source license may be exempt from certain obligations, and describe how adherence to the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice bears on enforcement. According to the Commission, the guidelines are not legally binding but set out its interpretation of the AI Act and will guide its enforcement; the page is the Commission's own announcement, and external assessment is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] European Commission — published guidelines on the scope of obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models under the AI Act
- [analysis] European Commission — states the guidelines aim to give legal certainty to actors across the AI value chain on when and how they must comply
- [fact] European Commission — says the guidelines complement the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, which it received from independent experts on 10 July
- [fact] European Commission — lists the baseline obligations as giving information to AI-system providers that integrate the model and putting in place a policy to comply with European copyright law
- [fact] European Commission — states that providers of general-purpose AI models presenting systemic risks must additionally assess and mitigate those risks, including risks to fundamental rights, safety, and loss of control over the model
- [fact] European Commission — defines a general-purpose AI model as one trained with more than 10^23 floating point operations of compute that can generate language (text or audio), text-to-image, or text-to-video
- [fact] European Commission — says the guidelines clarify when an actor modifying a general-purpose AI model is considered to become its provider, a question relevant to [[FineTuning]]
- [fact] European Commission — says the guidelines set the conditions under which providers of models released under a free and open-source license ([[ModelLicensing]]) and meeting certain transparency conditions may be exempt from certain AI Act obligations, the point where [[OpenSourceAI]] status carries regulatory weight
- [fact] European Commission — states that the obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models enter into application on 2 August 2025
- [fact] European Commission — states that providers of models to be classified as general-purpose AI models with systemic risk must notify the AI Office without delay
- [forecast] European Commission — says the AI Office will work closely with providers in the first year, in particular those adhering to the Code of Practice, to help them comply
- [fact] European Commission — states that its enforcement powers enter into application from 2 August 2026
- [fact] European Commission — states that providers whose models were on the market before 2 August 2025 must comply by 2 August 2027
- [fact] European Commission — says the guidelines were developed through a public consultation with input from hundreds of stakeholders and from an expert pool set up by its Joint Research Centre
- [analysis] European Commission — states the guidelines are not legally binding but set out its interpretation and application of the AI Act and will guide its enforcement actions
