## Summary

A TechCrunch report by Rebecca Bellan reads a Wednesday letter from [[Meta]] CEO [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] on "personal superintelligence" as a signal that Meta may keep its most advanced models closed. Zuckerberg wrote that superintelligence will raise novel safety concerns and that Meta must be "careful about what we choose to open source." TechCrunch contrasts this with his [[open-source-ai-path-forward|2024 open-source letter]], which predicted Llama would become the industry's most advanced model family. It ties the shift to Meta's June 2025 AGI sprint and to unnamed reports that Meta paused testing of its Llama Behemoth model. A Meta spokesperson answered that the company's position on [[OpenSourceAI|open source AI]] is unchanged and that it expects to train a mix of open and closed models. The raw gives no date in its frontmatter; the URL dates the piece to 2025-07-30.

## Key Quotes

> "We believe the benefits of superintelligence should be shared with the world as broadly as possible. That said, superintelligence will raise novel safety concerns. We'll need to be rigorous about mitigating these risks and careful about what we choose to open source." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO

> "If at some point however there's some qualitative change in what the thing is capable of, and we feel like it's not responsible to open source it, then we won't." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO, on a podcast

> "Personal devices like glasses that understand our context because they can see what we see, hear what we hear, and interact with us throughout the day will become our primary computing devices." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO

> "Our position on open source AI is unchanged. We plan to continue releasing leading open source models. We haven't released everything we've developed historically and we expect to continue training a mix of open and closed models going forward." — Meta spokesperson
