## Summary

A 22 March 2025 VentureBeat guest post by Jason Corso, a University of Michigan professor and co-founder of Voxel51, argues that calling an AI system open source is misleading unless every component that built it is shared. Corso lists those components as system source code, model parameters, dataset, hyperparameters, training source code, random number generation and software frameworks. He singles out [[Meta]]'s description of Llama 3.1 405B as "the first frontier-level open-source AI model," noting that Meta shared only the weights and some software while the source code and dataset stayed closed. He credits [[OpenWeights|open-weight]] releases such as [[DeepSeek]]'s R1 with real value, and cites the LAION-5B dataset case as evidence that full transparency lets outsiders find and fix problems. The piece is an opinion column and carries no response from Meta.

## Key Claims

- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that "open source" is being used haphazardly, and sometimes dishonestly, by tech companies to build trust in their AI releases
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that true open-source collaboration is a third way between the innovation and regulation camps, one already proven in earlier waves of technology
- [analysis] Jason Corso — states that open-source offerings Linux, Apache, MySQL and PHP unleashed the internet as it is known today
- [fact] Jason Corso — reports that an IBM study of 2,400 IT decision-makers found growing interest in using open-source AI tools to drive ROI
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that the transparency of open source allows independent scrutiny and auditing of AI systems' behaviors and ethics
- [fact] Jason Corso — reports that outside scrutiny found more than 1,000 URLs containing verified child sexual abuse material in the LAION-5B dataset, which fuels image generators such as Stable Diffusion and Midjourney
- [fact] Jason Corso — reports that LAION-5B's creators partnered with industry watchdogs and released a fixed dataset, RE-LAION 5B
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that had LAION-5B been closed, as with [[OpenAI]]'s Sora or Google's Gemini, the consequences could have been far worse
- [analysis] Jason Corso — explains that an AI system depends on system source code, model parameters, dataset, hyperparameters, training source code, random number generation and software frameworks working together
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that calling a release open source is accurate only if every component is shared so others can reproduce, modify and extend it
- [fact] [[Meta]] — described Llama 3.1 405B as "the first frontier-level open-source AI model"
- [fact] Jason Corso — reports that Meta publicly shared only Llama 3.1 405B's pre-trained weights and some software, keeping the source code and dataset closed
- [analysis] Jason Corso — acknowledges that open-weight models offer flexibility, accessibility, innovation and a level of transparency
- [fact] Jason Corso — reports that [[DeepSeek]] released R1's weights and technical reports and made the model free to use
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that DeepSeek's R1 release let the AI community study and verify its methodology
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that partial openness forces innovators to blindly trust the components that are not shared
- [analysis] Jason Corso — argues that AI benchmarking is insufficient because core datasets keep changing and appropriate metrics vary by use case
- [forecast] Jason Corso — warns that without self-governance by tech companies, the industry's transparency gap could hurt public trust and acceptance of AI

## Key Quotes

> "It is misleading, however, to call an AI system open source when no one can actually look at, experiment with and understand each piece of the puzzle that went into creating it." — Jason Corso, University of Michigan professor and Voxel51 co-founder

> "For this to be accurate, however, innovators must share all the pieces of the puzzle so that other players can fully understand, analyze and assess the AI system's properties to ultimately reproduce, modify and extend its capabilities." — Jason Corso, University of Michigan professor and Voxel51 co-founder

> "the first frontier-level open-source AI model" — [[Meta]], on Llama 3.1 405B

> "Embracing openness, transparency and open source is not just a strong business model — it's also about choosing between an AI future that benefits everyone instead of just the few." — Jason Corso, University of Michigan professor and Voxel51 co-founder
