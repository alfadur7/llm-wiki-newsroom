## Summary

A Stanford HAI news piece reports that Chinese [[OpenWeights|open-weight]] models such as [[MoonshotAI|Moonshot AI]]'s Kimi K3 and Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max are closing the capability gap with top American models. It sets that against a US policy debate over frontier-AI risk, in which a Commerce Department export-control order took [[Anthropic]]'s Mythos 5 and Fable 5 offline. Stanford HAI Denning Director James Landay argues that open weights amount only to "open distribution" and that [[OpenSourceAI|open source AI]] must also release code, [[TrainingData|training data]] or an auditable account of it, and tooling. He ties that bar to the top "Open Science" class of the Linux Foundation's [[ModelOpennessFramework|Model Openness Framework]]. He warns that closed frontier models concentrate power, and he proposes decentralized AI, data portability and verifiable agents, with universities building open systems. The page carries no author byline and no publication date.

## Key Claims

- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that its AI Index found the US-China AI performance gap narrowing to a few percentage points last year (compare [[beyond-deepseek-china-open-weight-ecosystem|Beyond DeepSeek]])
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — says offerings from [[DeepSeek]] and Alibaba have closed much of the performance gap between Chinese open-weight models and American models
- [fact] Stanford HAI — describes Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 as having a million-token context window, giving its size as both 2.8 trillion and 2.4 trillion parameters in the same sentence
- [fact] Stanford HAI — states that Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max has 2.4 trillion parameters
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — calls Kimi K3 and Qwen3.8-Max the most powerful open-weight models yet, edging closer in the rankings to the best American models while costing less
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — says the current US administration has shifted from an "innovation first" position to a more reactive one that requires advance notice of new model releases
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that a Commerce Department export-control order forced [[Anthropic]] to take Mythos 5 and Fable 5 fully offline
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — says the administration is weighing whether to extend that scrutiny to Chinese open models
- [fact] Stanford HAI — reports that Nvidia, Microsoft, [[Meta]] and more than 20 other companies published an open letter warning Washington against "premature restrictions" on open-weight models
- [analysis] James Landay — agrees with the instinct behind the companies' letter but argues that it stops short of the point
- [analysis] James Landay — argues that open weights let users download a model and run it on their own machine but not see how it was built or what it was trained on, which he calls "open distribution"
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — explains that an AI artifact bundles software, training and test data, and learned parameters, each of which can be open or closed independently
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — says a company can release the weights, withhold the data and training code, and still call the result "open"
- [analysis] James Landay — argues that outsiders can test, reproduce, or challenge a model only if companies hand over the [[TrainingData|training data]]
- [analysis] James Landay — says Stanford HAI aligns its bar with the top "Open Science" class of the Linux Foundation's [[ModelOpennessFramework|Model Openness Framework]]
- [analysis] James Landay — defines that bar as releasing the code, the training data or a thoroughly documented, auditable account of it, and the tooling, with a real way for outsiders to download, run, study, contribute to, and modify the work
- [analysis] James Landay — argues that anything short of that bar forecloses research on how training data shapes a model's cultural assumptions, on fair representation of populations, and on new architectures at scale
- [analysis] James Landay — says the transformer, attention mechanisms, and mixture-of-experts routing all came out of published research that others could examine and build on
- [analysis] James Landay — argues that closed models are not necessarily less safe in the moment but concentrate control over access, price, and embedded values in a small number of firms
- [analysis] James Landay — likens closed platforms to what Cory Doctorow calls "enshittification," in which platforms pivot from creating value for users to extracting it
- [analysis] James Landay — argues that the longer a user stays inside a closed model's platform, the more context it accumulates and the higher the cost of leaving
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — argues that closed models can be breached, misused, or fail in ways outsiders cannot detect, so concentrating frontier capability concentrates those blind spots
- [analysis] Stanford HAI — argues that a handful of US or Chinese labs training on their own data and language risk carrying one culture's worldview into everyone else's daily life
- [analysis] James Landay — points to [[Anthropic]]'s latest models going dark under the Commerce Department order as a preview of how access to closed frontier AI can become a bargaining chip in trade or export-control fights
- [analysis] James Landay — proposes decentralized AI that users run locally or on their own servers
- [analysis] James Landay — proposes data portability so that users' context and data travel with them between platforms
- [analysis] James Landay — proposes verifiable agents whose builders, loyalties, and actions can be checked
- [analysis] James Landay — argues that universities should build and study leading-edge open systems because they can work on longer timelines and publish work meant to be checked
- [forecast] James Landay — warns that if "open" means only "downloadable," the world will have traded one set of closed labs for another under a different flag

## Key Quotes

> "Open weights are progress. You can download the model, run it on your own machine, keep it out of someone else's data pipeline. But you still can't see how the thing was built, what it was trained on, or why it behaves the way it does. That's not an open model. That's open distribution." — James Landay, Denning Director, Stanford HAI

> "There's a wide gap between open-weight AI and open source AI." — James Landay, Denning Director, Stanford HAI

> "Open weights answer 'Can I run this?' Open source answers 'Can I trust this, improve it, and build the next thing on top of it?' Right now almost everyone – American labs and Chinese labs alike – is answering the first question but nowhere close to the second." — James Landay, Denning Director, Stanford HAI

> "A closed frontier model behind an API is the ultimate walled garden. You don't own your data, your history with the model, or any leverage to leave. That's already the norm." — James Landay, Denning Director, Stanford HAI

> "If the future is genuinely open, in the full sense, it doesn't matter as much whose flag is on the release, because anyone anywhere can inspect it, adapt it, and hold it accountable. If 'open' just means 'downloadable,' we've traded one set of closed labs for another. Same concentration of power, different flag." — James Landay, Denning Director, Stanford HAI
