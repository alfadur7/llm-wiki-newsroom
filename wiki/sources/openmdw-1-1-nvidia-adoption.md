## Summary

The Linux Foundation announced on 2026-05-28 the release of OpenMDW-1.1, an updated permissive license framework written specifically for AI model distributions. According to the announcement, NVIDIA plans to adopt it across future releases of its Cosmos, Isaac GR00T, Ising, and Nemotron open model families. The Linux Foundation says OpenMDW, first launched in 2025 with the PyTorch Foundation, fills a gap left by software licenses not designed for AI artifacts and by custom AI licenses with restrictive usage terms. This is a Linux Foundation press release quoting only the Linux Foundation and NVIDIA; external assessment of the license is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] Linux Foundation — released OpenMDW-1.1 on 2026-05-28 as an updated open license framework designed specifically for AI model distributions
- [fact] NVIDIA — plans to adopt OpenMDW-1.1 across future releases of its Cosmos, Isaac GR00T, Ising, and Nemotron open model families
- [analysis] Linux Foundation — describes the four NVIDIA model families as spanning agentic AI, quantum computing, robotics, and simulation
- [analysis] Linux Foundation — characterizes OpenMDW as a model-centric, permissive license that gives models and their related artifacts one unified legal framework under [[ModelLicensing]]
- [fact] Linux Foundation — states that the OpenMDW license covers models (architecture, weights, and parameters), code (object code, source code, or scripts), documentation, and data
- [analysis] Linux Foundation — says the adoption will give developers rights to train, modify, contribute, redistribute, and deploy open models
- [fact] Linux Foundation — states that it launched OpenMDW in 2025 together with the PyTorch Foundation
- [analysis] Linux Foundation — argues that many [[OpenWeights]] models carry either traditional open source software licenses not designed for AI artifacts or custom AI licenses that imposed restrictive usage limitations
- [forecast] Linux Foundation — frames the NVIDIA adoption as a signal of growing momentum toward unified, permissive licensing standards for AI models
- [fact] Jim Zemlin — said that adopting OpenMDW gives organizations greater confidence to build, contribute, and deploy AI systems in the open [[openmdw-1-1-nvidia-adoption#Key Quotes]]
- [fact] Kari Briski — said that NVIDIA's adoption of OpenMDW helps establish a simpler, more consistent standard for open models at scale [[openmdw-1-1-nvidia-adoption#Key Quotes]]
- [fact] Linux Foundation — states that OpenMDW-1.1 is publicly available for adoption by AI model providers and developers worldwide
