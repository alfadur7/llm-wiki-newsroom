## Summary

In a February 18, 2025 opinion post by Jordan Maris, the [[OpenSourceInitiative]] (OSI) states that the licenses [[Meta]] attached to the Llama 3.x model series still fail the Open Source Definition (OSD), a year after the OSI said the same of Llama 2. The OSI says it agrees with a recent [[FreeSoftwareFoundation]] evaluation that the Llama 3.1 Community License fails freedom 0 and OSD points 5 and 6, and adds that newer Llama licenses exclude persons in the European Union. The post rejects claims by unnamed individuals that the Open Source AI Definition (OSAID) caused confusion about Llama, and calls on the community to press Meta to change the license. The post is the OSI's own position; Meta's response is outside the raw's scope.

- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that Llama 3.x, released under new licensing terms since its call on Meta a year earlier, still fails the Open Source Definition [[osi-meta-llama-2-license-not-open-source#Key Claims|OSI's 2023 Llama 2 post]]
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says Meta keeps falsely promoting Llama as "Open Source"

- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — agrees with the Free Software Foundation's recent evaluation that the Llama 3.1 Community License fails freedom 0, the freedom to use the model for any purpose
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that the Llama 3.1 Community License fails OSD point 5 by discriminating against users
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that the Llama 3.1 Community License fails OSD point 6 by restricting fields of endeavour
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says these restrictions were already present in the Llama 2 license and all remain in the newer licenses
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that newer versions of the Llama community license exclude any persons in the European Union from using the model, without explanation
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues the OSAID created no confusion about Llama's proprietary nature, since Meta's open washing and the OSI's response to it predate the OSAID
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says unnamed individuals who tie their criticism of the OSAID to Meta's use of the term are supporting Meta's open washing and sowing fear, uncertainty, and doubt
- [forecast] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says it will keep fighting open washing through public statements, events, and education aimed at the Open Source and AI communities, lawmakers, and businesses

> "The OSAID did not create any confusion about the proprietary nature of Llama; in fact, Meta's open washing, and our response to it, predate the OSAID entirely." — Jordan Maris, author of the OSI opinion post

> "At a time when Meta is trying to redefine Open Source for their own benefit and at the expense of our freedom, we call on the whole Open Source community to unite and call out Meta's open washing." — Jordan Maris, author of the OSI opinion post
