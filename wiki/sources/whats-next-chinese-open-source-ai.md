## Summary

An MIT Technology Review "What's Next" piece argues that Chinese [[OpenWeights|open-weight]] models, from [[DeepSeek]]'s R1 in January 2025 to [[MoonshotAI|Moonshot AI]]'s Kimi K2.5, now approach leading Western models at a fraction of the price and have overtaken US models in download counts. It makes three forecasts: China's commitment to releasing open models will continue, the next wave of models will be narrower and more specialized, and Chinese open models will become infrastructure for AI builders worldwide, including in Silicon Valley. The piece draws on interviews with Tsinghua professor Liu Zhiyuan, [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]]'s Tiezhen Wang and Atoms founder Alex Chenglin Wu, and on adoption figures from Hugging Face, OpenRouter and Nathan Lambert's ATOM project. It uses "open-source" for releases it describes as publishing weights, and does not weigh them against a license or completeness test.

## Key Claims

- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that Moonshot AI released the open-weight model Kimi K2.5, which came close to [[Anthropic]]'s Claude Opus on some early benchmarks at roughly one-seventh of Opus's price
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that Alibaba's Qwen family, after ranking as the most downloaded model series in 2025 and 2026, has overtaken [[Meta]]'s Llama models in cumulative downloads on [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]]
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — cites a recent MIT study finding that Chinese open-source models have surpassed US models in total downloads
- [analysis] MIT Technology Review — explains that the Chinese companies publish their models' weights so anyone can download, run, study and modify them, unlike paid, uninspectable US models such as ChatGPT or Claude
- [forecast] MIT Technology Review — predicts that if these open models keep improving, they will change where innovation happens and who sets the standards
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that [[DeepSeek]] released R1 as an open-weight model under the permissive MIT License and published a paper detailing its training process
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that DeepSeek offered API access at a fraction of the cost of [[OpenAI]]'s o1, then the leading proprietary reasoning model
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that within days of release DeepSeek replaced ChatGPT as the most downloaded free app in the US App Store and triggered a sell-off that briefly erased roughly $1 trillion in US tech market value
- [analysis] MIT Technology Review — assesses that an open-source strategy was seen in China as the fastest way to close the gap with US labs by rallying developers, spreading adoption and setting standards
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that by September 2024 Alibaba said global Qwen downloads had exceeded 600 million, and that Qwen accounted for more than 30% of all Hugging Face model downloads in 2024
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence and Baichuan were releasing open models as early as 2023
- [analysis] MIT Technology Review — observes that capabilities which once took months to reach the open-source world now emerge within weeks or days as Z.ai, MiniMax, Tencent and smaller labs release competitive models
- [analysis] Liu Zhiyuan — says Chinese AI firms gain reputation and free publicity by releasing strong research, and that open source has become "politically correct" in the Chinese programmer community as a response to US dominance in proprietary AI
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that in August China's State Council released a draft policy encouraging universities to reward open-source work, proposing that students' contributions on GitHub or Gitee could count toward academic credit
- [forecast] Tiezhen Wang — expects China's push for open models to continue in the near term, with long-term sustainability hinging on financial results
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that the model labs Z.ai and MiniMax went public in Hong Kong in January
- [analysis] MIT Technology Review — describes Qwen as the first open model family to roll out enough high-quality variants, from laptop-sized to multi-hundred-billion-parameter models, to feel like a free full product line
- [fact] Nathan Lambert — reports through his ATOM (American Truly Open Models) project that by 2025-08-04 Qwen-derived variants were "more than 40%" of new Hugging Face language-model derivatives, while Llama had fallen to about 15%
- [analysis] Liu Zhiyuan — argues that the rise of small models is about making AI cheaper to run and easier for more people to use, since compute and energy constrain any deployment
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that the team behind the open-source agent OpenClaw said Kimi K2.5 surpassed Claude Opus to become its most used model by token count within days of OpenClaw's release
- [analysis] Tiezhen Wang — suggests it would be a mistake to treat Chinese models as mere "dupes" of Western frontier systems, since a model only needs to be good enough for the job at hand
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that Shanghai AI Laboratory released science-oriented models, Tencent projects focused on music generation, and Ubiquant released an open model for medical reasoning
- [analysis] MIT Technology Review — reports that DeepSeek's published work on compressing the attention cache to cut memory and inference costs has drawn significant attention in the research community
- [fact] Martin Casado — stated in a post on X that among startups pitching with open-source stacks there is about an 80% chance they run on Chinese open models
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that OpenRouter usage data show Chinese open models rising from almost none in late 2024 to nearly 30% of usage in some recent weeks
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports, citing CNBC, that Z.ai limited new subscriptions to its GLM coding plan over compute constraints, with users concentrated in the United States and China, followed by India, Japan, Brazil and the UK
- [analysis] Tiezhen Wang — says the open-source ecosystems in China and the US are tightly bound together
- [fact] MIT Technology Review — reports that many Chinese open models still rely on Nvidia and US cloud platforms to train and serve them
- [fact] Dario Amodei — wrote after DeepSeek's 2025 releases that export controls are "not a way to duck the competition" between the US and China and that US AI companies "must have better models" to prevail
- [analysis] MIT Technology Review — notes that the export this time is the underlying model layer others build on, and calls it an open question whether it will escape the scrutiny and restrictions earlier Chinese tech met in the West

## Key Quotes

> "Thirty years ago, no Chinese person would believe they could be at the center of global innovation. DeepSeek shows that with solid technical talent, a supportive environment, and the right organizational culture, it's possible to do truly world-class work." — Alex Chenglin Wu, CEO and founder of Atoms

> "Chinese AI firms have seen real gains from the open-source playbook. By releasing strong research, they build reputation and gain free publicity." — Liu Zhiyuan, Tsinghua University professor and chief scientist at ModelBest

> "In the Chinese programmer community, open source has become politically correct." — Liu Zhiyuan, Tsinghua University professor and chief scientist at ModelBest

> "Compute and energy are real constraints for any deployment." — Liu Zhiyuan, Tsinghua University professor and chief scientist at ModelBest

> "Right now, the focus is on making the cake bigger. The next challenge is figuring out how each company secures its share." — Tiezhen Wang, Hugging Face

> "The impact of these research breakthroughs is amplified because they're open-sourced and can be picked up quickly across the field." — Tiezhen Wang, Hugging Face

> "The open-source ecosystems in China and the US are tightly bound together." — Tiezhen Wang, Hugging Face
