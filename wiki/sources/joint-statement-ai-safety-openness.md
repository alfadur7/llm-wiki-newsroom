## Summary

A joint statement hosted on a [[Mozilla]] site argues that openness, transparency, and broad access are the path to AI safety, not a threat to it. The signatories concede that openly available models carry risks, but they call tight proprietary control of foundation models "naive at best, dangerous at worst" and warn that hasty regulation can concentrate power. They ask for investment in a spectrum of approaches, from open source to open science. The page lists dozens of named signatories, including staff of [[Mozilla]], [[Meta]], and the [[OpenSourceInitiative]], and links to a total of 1821 signatures. The statement carries no publication date.

## Key Claims

- [analysis] Joint Statement signatories — hold that openness, transparency, and broad access are needed to mitigate current and future harms from AI systems, and call this a global priority
- [analysis] Joint Statement signatories — acknowledge that openly available models can be abused by malicious actors or deployed by ill-equipped developers
- [analysis] Joint Statement signatories — argue that proprietary technologies carry the same risks, and that public access and scrutiny make technology safer
- [analysis] Joint Statement signatories — reject the idea that tight proprietary control of foundation models is the only path to protection from society-scale harm
- [analysis] Joint Statement signatories — warn that rushing toward the wrong kind of regulation can concentrate power in ways that hurt competition and innovation
- [analysis] Joint Statement signatories — say the ongoing debate over what "open" means in AI should speed experimentation rather than slow it
- [forecast] Joint Statement signatories — expect investment in approaches from open source to open science to accelerate independent research on AI risks, help regulators monitor large AI systems, and lower entry barriers for responsible new players
- [fact] Joint Statement signatories — describe themselves as holding divergent views on how [[OpenSourceAI]] should be managed and released
- [fact] Joint Statement signatories — include Mark Surman, Mitchell Baker, Ayah Bdeir, and Imo Udom of [[Mozilla]]
- [fact] Joint Statement signatories — include Yann LeCun and Joelle Pineau, both listed with [[Meta]]
- [fact] Joint Statement signatories — include Stefano Maffulli, listed with the [[OpenSourceInitiative]]
- [fact] Joint Statement signatories — include Arthur Mensch, Cedric O, and Charles Gorintin, all listed with MistralAI

## Key Quotes

> "The idea that tight and proprietary control of foundational AI models is the only path to protecting us from society-scale harm is naive at best, dangerous at worst." — Joint Statement signatories

> "If our objectives are safety, security and accountability, then openness and transparency are essential ingredients to get us there." — Joint Statement signatories

> "We are in the midst of a dynamic discourse about what 'open' signifies in the AI era. This important debate should not slow us down." — Joint Statement signatories

> "When it comes to AI safety and security, openness is an antidote, not a poison." — Joint Statement signatories
