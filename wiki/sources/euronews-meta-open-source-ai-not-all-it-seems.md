## Summary

Euronews Next's Pascale Davies reports, on the day the [[OpenSourceInitiative]] (OSI) set its final Open Source AI Definition, that the definition does not match [[Meta]]'s use of the "open source" label for Llama. OSI head [[StefanoMaffulli]] says Meta's terms of use and distribution are incompatible with open source principles and that withheld [[TrainingData]] and training code are where the innovation sits. The article sets this against [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]]'s claims that open source AI is becoming the industry standard, and notes that Meta did not reply to a request for comment.

## Key Claims

- [fact] Pascale Davies — reports that the OSI set a final definition for open source AI on Monday, 2024-10-28
- [fact] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — said Meta and some other AI firms fail the definition because their terms of use and distribution are incompatible with the [[OpenSourceDefinition|open source definition]] and principles
- [analysis] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — compared these firms to Microsoft, Oracle and Adobe, platforms that invite others to build on them while calling themselves open
- [fact] Pascale Davies — reports that the OSI spent a couple of years on the definition and consulted a 70-person group of researchers, lawyers, policymakers, activists and representatives of Microsoft, Meta and Google
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that an open source AI can be used for any reason without the company's permission and that researchers should be able to see freely how the system works
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that the AI system can be modified for any purpose, including changing its output, and shared with or without modifications
- [analysis] Pascale Davies — reports that Llama 3.1 is partially open source under the OSI definition, because developers can download it for free and customize it
- [analysis] Pascale Davies — reports that Meta does not specify where the Llama 3.1 training data came from, which could lead to copyright issues or biased data
- [analysis] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — said companies that do name their data sources are often vague and just say "the Internet"
- [analysis] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — said the real innovation lies in how datasets pass through the training machinery, and that companies do not want to release that code
- [forecast] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — warned that blurring which models are truly open source may hamper long-term development of user-controlled AI and leave the edge with a handful of companies
- [fact] Pascale Davies — reports that Meta did not reply to Euronews Next's request for comment before publication
- [fact] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — wrote that Meta is "taking the next steps towards open source AI becoming the industry standard" and that Llama has been "a bedrock of AI innovation globally" [[open-source-ai-path-forward#Key Quotes]]
- [fact] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — said Microsoft and Google retracted the open source term for models that were not fully open, while talks with Meta produced no result
- [analysis] Pascale Davies — reports experts' view that confusion around the term leads to open-washing, where companies promote open models without contributing to the commons
- [fact] [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]] — said in a February earnings call that open source software often becomes an industry standard and that companies standardizing on Meta's stack make it easier to integrate new innovations into Meta's products
- [forecast] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — expects AI to differ from the largely unregulated early social-media era because regulators are already watching and regulating
- [analysis] Pascale Davies — reports that the OSI has no strong power to enforce the definition, but that judges and courts are starting to recognize the open source definition in mergers and regulation
- [forecast] [[StefanoMaffulli]] — said the OSI expects the definition to have an impact on regulators

## Key Quotes

> "They fail, especially Meta, because their terms of use and terms of distribution are incompatible with the open source definition and the open source principles." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], OSI head

> "They're basically the Microsoft, the Oracle, the Adobe of this space where they say 'build on top of my platform don't worry about it and I'll keep on getting grants from you using our platforms. But they also say 'it's open, so everyone can use it.'" — [[StefanoMaffulli]], OSI head

> "If you talk to companies, they don't want to release that code." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], OSI head

> "I fear that society as a whole would be in a worse place if we let a handful of companies just go on and be the only ones who have the edge and the access to innovation this way." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], OSI head

> "Open source software often becomes an industry standard, and when companies standardise on building with our stack, that then becomes easier to integrate new innovations into our products." — [[MarkZuckerberg|Mark Zuckerberg]], Meta CEO

> "We do expect the definition to have an impact on regulators. They're watching us. We have become credible interlocutors." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], OSI head
