## Summary

A Nature news article by Elizabeth Gibney, republished by Scientific American on 2025-09-17, reports on the peer-reviewed version of [[DeepSeek]]'s R1 reasoning model published in Nature that day. The paper's supplementary material puts R1's training cost at about US$294,000, on top of roughly $6 million for the base model, and DeepSeek states R1 was trained mainly on Nvidia H800 chips. In exchanges with referees, DeepSeek stated that R1 did not learn by copying reasoning examples generated by [[OpenAI]] models, answering January media reports that OpenAI researchers suspected [[Distillation|distillation]] from OpenAI outputs. DeepSeek has not published R1's [[TrainingData|training data]]; the two outside researchers quoted, one of whom reviewed the paper, call the peer review a welcome precedent and the rebuttal convincing.

## Key Claims

- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that the peer-reviewed version of DeepSeek's R1 model was published in Nature on 2025-09-17, updating a preprint released in January
- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that R1, as an open-weight model, is available for anyone to download and has been downloaded 10.9 million times on [[HuggingFace|Hugging Face]], the most of any such model on the platform
- [fact] [[DeepSeek]] — disclosed in the paper's supplementary material that training R1 cost the equivalent of US$294,000
- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that the R1 training cost comes on top of about $6 million that DeepSeek spent to make the base LLM R1 is built on
- [analysis] Elizabeth Gibney — assesses the combined cost as substantially less than the tens of millions of dollars that rival models are thought to have cost
- [fact] [[DeepSeek]] — states that R1 was trained mainly on Nvidia H800 chips, which US export controls barred from sale to China in 2023
- [analysis] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that R1 is thought to be the first major LLM to undergo peer review
- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that, in response to peer-review comments, the DeepSeek team reduced anthropomorphizing and added clarifications on the model's training data and safety
- [fact] [[DeepSeek]] — states that R1 was created with pure reinforcement learning that rewarded correct answers rather than teaching the model to follow human-selected reasoning examples
- [analysis] [[DeepSeek]] — says R1 learned its own reasoning-like strategies, such as verifying its workings, without human-prescribed tactics
- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that R1 scored its own attempts using estimates rather than a separate algorithm, a technique called group relative policy optimization
- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that January media reports suggested [[OpenAI]] researchers thought DeepSeek had used outputs from OpenAI models to train R1
- [fact] [[DeepSeek]] — stated in exchanges with referees that R1 did not learn by copying reasoning examples generated by OpenAI models
- [fact] [[DeepSeek]] — acknowledged that R1's base model was trained on the web, so it will have ingested AI-generated content already on the Internet
- [fact] Elizabeth Gibney — reports that DeepSeek has not published its training data as part of the paper
- [analysis] Huan Sun — assesses DeepSeek's rebuttal on OpenAI outputs as "as convincing as what we could see in any publication"
- [analysis] Lewis Tunstall — says he cannot be fully sure R1 was not trained on OpenAI examples, but that replication attempts by other labs suggest DeepSeek's recipe does not need them
- [analysis] Huan Sun — says almost all 2025 work on reinforcement learning in LLMs might have been inspired by R1
- [fact] Huan Sun — reports that on ScienceAgentBench Sun and colleagues found R1 was not first for accuracy but was one of the best models at balancing ability with cost
- [analysis] Lewis Tunstall — says other researchers are applying R1's methods to improve existing LLMs' reasoning and extend it beyond mathematics and coding

## Key Quotes

> "This is a very welcome precedent." — Lewis Tunstall, machine-learning engineer at Hugging Face and reviewer of the Nature paper

> "If we don't have this norm of sharing a large part of this process publicly, it becomes very hard to evaluate whether these systems pose risks or not." — Lewis Tunstall, machine-learning engineer at Hugging Face

> "Going through a rigorous peer-review process certainly helps verify the validity and usefulness of the model. Other firms should do the same." — Huan Sun, AI researcher at Ohio State University

> "Almost all work in 2025 so far that conducts reinforcement learning in LLMs might have been inspired by R1 one way or another." — Huan Sun, AI researcher at Ohio State University

> "I think the evidence now is fairly clear that you can get very high performance just using pure reinforcement learning." — Lewis Tunstall, machine-learning engineer at Hugging Face
