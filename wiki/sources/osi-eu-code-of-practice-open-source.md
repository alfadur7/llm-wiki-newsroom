## Summary

In a 2025-03-27 blog post by Jordan Maris, the [[OpenSourceInitiative|Open Source Initiative]] (OSI) says early drafts of the EU Code of Practice for General Purpose AI, the voluntary rules drafted under the [[EUAIAct|EU AI Act]], would have made compliance impossible for [[OpenSourceAI|Open Source AI]] projects. According to the OSI, the drafts mandated acceptable use policies and prohibitions on certain uses, which it says conflict with rule 6 of the [[OpenSourceDefinition|Open Source Definition]] (No Discrimination Against Fields of Endeavor). The OSI states that after it and like-minded organizations wrote to the drafting chairs, the third draft of 11 March made acceptable use policies optional and exempted Open Source AI from prohibiting certain downstream uses. The post is the OSI's own account of its advocacy; the drafting chairs have no comment in it, and external assessment of the OSI's role is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that the EU approved the Artificial Intelligence Act in 2024, and describes it as the world's first legal framework for AI
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says the AI Act mandated the creation of a Code of Practice for General Purpose AI for AI developers
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — describes the Code of Practice as a set of voluntary rules to help developers prove they comply with the AI Act, a temporary measure while the EU develops in-depth AI standards that the Code will inspire
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that it applied in August 2024 to take part in drafting the Code of Practice and was accepted
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — assesses that the Code of Practice overall proposes sensible practices to reduce the risks AI could pose
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says AI systems that comply with its Open Source AI Definition already follow most of the Code's rules
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that previous drafts of the Code mandated acceptable use policies and a prohibition of certain uses of the AI system
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues that such use restrictions conflict with rule 6 of the Open Source Definition, No Discrimination Against Fields of Endeavor, so developers would have had to choose between complying with the Code and being Open Source
- [forecast] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues that companies might not consider Open Source solutions if unsure they can comply with the law, which could delay or halt the development and deployment of Open Source AI systems in Europe
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — calls Open Source AI systems by nature the most transparent and accessible
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues the proposed restrictions cannot be enforced, since a downstream user could remove restrictions and acceptable use policies could simply be ignored
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that it raised the incompatibility with Open Source principles in the first round of feedback
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that after seeing no improvement in the second draft, it teamed up with like-minded organizations to write a letter to the drafting chairs
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — reports that the third draft of the Code, released on 11 March, made acceptable use policies optional
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — reports that the third draft exempted Open Source AI from prohibiting certain downstream uses
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — welcomes the changes as removing a serious barrier to Open Source AI development in Europe by letting Open Source AI developers adhere to the Code
- [forecast] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says it will keep following the drafting process to ensure the changes are not reversed
- [forecast] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says it will continue to fight the openwashing of some AI models, naming [[Meta]]'s Llama

## Key Quotes

> "This would mean developers would have to choose between complying with the Code of Practice or being Open Source." — Jordan Maris, OSI

> "Additionally, the proposed restrictions can't actually be enforced; restrictions could be removed by a downstream user, and acceptable use policies could simply be ignored." — Jordan Maris, OSI

> "We'll also continue to fight the openwashing of some AI models such as Meta's Llama, while working to educate and inform lawmakers about Open Source and its benefits." — Jordan Maris, OSI
