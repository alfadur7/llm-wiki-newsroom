## Summary

In a post dated 2026-07-27, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei states that Anthropic has never advocated a ban on [[OpenWeights|open-weights]] models. He writes it in response to reports that some US officials were weighing a ban on Chinese open-weights models, and to an industry letter defending open weights. Amodei calls open-weights models without dangerous capabilities a public good and says protectionist bans would not address his two main national-security concerns. Instead he backs three measures: no powerful chips for China, a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation, and mandatory safety testing for all sufficiently capable models, open and closed. He agrees with much of the industry letter, but he rejects its claims that open weights necessarily make safeguards easier or help defenders more than attackers. The raw does not name that letter; its points match the letter on [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership|the open weights letter page]]. The post states only Anthropic's own position, and external assessment is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] Dario Amodei — states that Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — calls open-weights models that lack dangerous capabilities a public good that costs nothing beyond the compute to run them
- [fact] Dario Amodei — reports that some US officials were said to be considering a ban on US companies using Chinese open-weights models
- [fact] Dario Amodei — says some people accused Anthropic of wanting to ban open-weights models to protect its business
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that protectionist bans would not address his most serious national-security concerns
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — names as his primary concern that authoritarian governments, the CCP most capably, build AI more powerful than the US and use it for permanent military superiority or deep repression
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that whether such authoritarian models are released with open weights is irrelevant to the primary concern
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — names as his secondary concern that powerful models may be misused for cyberattacks or biological attacks, or may have serious alignment problems
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that open-weights models potentially carry higher risk than closed models, because guardrails and usage monitoring are hard to apply and released weights cannot be withdrawn
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that banning US businesses from using open-weights models does nothing about misuse risk, because bad actors are unlikely to be legitimate US businesses
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — says such a ban would protect US AI companies from competition, and that this has never been his goal
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — urges that powerful chips and chipmaking equipment not be sold to China, and that chip smuggling and workarounds be curbed
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that China's limited domestic chip production means, under scaling laws, it cannot build more powerful models than the US without US chips
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — urges a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation operations, which he says let China partially evade chip bans
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that distillation can bring the Chinese frontier to within a few months of the US frontier, but not to equal or better capability
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that the state backing of distillation operations matters far more than the fact that many of them release open-weights models
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — urges mandatory safety testing for all sufficiently capable models, open and closed, for cyber, biological, and alignment risks before release
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — says the Trump administration has moved toward such testing in recent months
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that whether open models pose more risk, and whether it can be mitigated, should emerge from testing rather than be decided in advance
- [fact] Dario Amodei — cites research from AE Studio and Anthropic on modular training strategies as a possible way to improve open-weights model safety
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — argues that effective safety testing would need to be global, including the CCP, and that this may be possible because it is in China's interest too
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — agrees with the industry letter that open weights expand access, strengthen competition for some use cases, and give customers more control [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership#Key Claims]]
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — agrees with the industry letter that distillation concerns should be handled through targeted legal and commercial frameworks [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership#Key Claims]]
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — rejects the industry letter's assertion that open-weights models necessarily make it easier to develop safeguards [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership#Key Claims]]
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — rejects the industry letter's assertion that broad access to capabilities necessarily helps defenders more than attackers [[open-weights-american-ai-leadership#Key Claims]]
- [forecast] Dario Amodei — expects biology to have a strong attacker-defender asymmetry, with capable models able to weaponize pandemic-level viruses quickly while defense takes years
- [fact] Dario Amodei — says Anthropic identifies and bans accounts that use its models for industrial-scale distillation
- [analysis] Dario Amodei — says distilling accounts can often be identified only after substantial distillation has occurred, and that no single company's practices can solve the problem
- [fact] Dario Amodei — quotes a UK AI Security Institute report stating that open-weight release of models with dangerous capabilities creates a persistent and irreversible risk of misuse

## Key Quotes

> "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models." — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO

> "Open-weights models that don't have dangerous capabilities are a public good: they don't cost anything besides the compute needed to run them, and they provide value to businesses, developers, and researchers." — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO

> "But banning the use of these models by US businesses does nothing to address this risk, because bad actors are unlikely to be legitimate US businesses. It would protect US AI companies from competition, but that has never been my goal." — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO

> "But I don't agree with the letter's assertions that open-weights models necessarily make it easier to develop safeguards or that broad access to capabilities necessarily helps defenders more than attackers. It seems at least as likely to me that the opposite will be true." — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO

> "Whether open models do or don't pose an increased risk, and whether that risk can be mitigated, is something that should emerge from testing, rather than be decided in advance" — Dario Amodei, Anthropic CEO

> "Once open-weight models are released, these options are lost permanently: safeguards can be removed, and copies can be downloaded, redistributed, and run on private systems beyond monitoring." — UK AI Security Institute report, as quoted by Dario Amodei
