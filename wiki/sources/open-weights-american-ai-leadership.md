## Summary

An open letter dated 2026-07-24 and hosted on a Microsoft corporate-responsibility page argues that [[OpenWeights|open weight]] models are an important part of sustaining American AI leadership. The signatories say open weights expand access, strengthen competition, give customers control, and can make AI safer through outside scrutiny. They concede that released weights are beyond the developer's control, but they ask policymakers not to prohibit open weights or restrict distillation. According to the page, more than 270 companies and organizations had signed by 2026-08-03, including [[Meta]], [[Mozilla]], and Microsoft. The page carries only the signatories' own case; external assessment of its claims is outside the raw's scope.

## Key Claims

- [fact] Open Weights letter signatories — define open weight models as AI models that anyone can download, inspect, modify, and run on their own infrastructure
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — compare the choice facing U.S. AI policy to the 1980s open-source software movement, which they say now supports most of the internet
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that U.S. AI leadership will be judged by whether the country builds a strong, open ecosystem, not by one frontier model
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that open weights let startups, universities, and public institutions build on advanced models without training one from scratch
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that open weights let organizations match the right model to each task and reserve frontier-scale capability for frontier problems
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that open weights create rivalry among model developers and across cloud, chips, applications, and services
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that open weights help organizations avoid lock-in to a single provider and keep control of their own data
- [fact] Open Weights letter signatories — acknowledge that released weights are beyond the original developer's control and that modified versions are difficult to trace or reverse
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that cybersecurity defenders need access to models with capabilities comparable to those attackers use
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that relying solely on closed models is not inherently safe because closed models can be breached, misused, or fail undetected
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that concentrating advanced AI behind a few closed models creates single points of failure
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — argue that open weight models let a broad community examine model behavior, identify vulnerabilities, and develop safeguards
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — ask policymakers to expand compute access for startups and researchers and to invest in shared datasets, tools, and evaluation frameworks
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — urge policymakers to avoid premature restrictions on open models that could stifle competition or drive innovation overseas
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — describe distillation, training one model on another model's outputs, as a widely used technique for model improvement, evaluation, and validation
- [analysis] Open Weights letter signatories — say unlawful extraction of value from closed models should be handled through targeted legal and commercial frameworks rather than sweeping restrictions on distillation
- [fact] Open Weights letter signatories — number more than 270 companies and organizations as of 2026-08-03, according to the letter page
- [fact] Open Weights letter signatories — include [[Meta]] and [[Mozilla]] in the published signatory list
- [fact] Open Weights letter signatories — include Microsoft, Google, OpenAI, NVIDIA, Amazon, IBM, Mistral, and Hugging Face in the published signatory list
- [fact] Open Weights letter signatories — include the Linux Foundation and EleutherAI in the published signatory list

## Key Quotes

> "Open weight models—AI models that anyone can download, inspect, modify, and run on their own infrastructure—are an important part of that foundation because they make advanced AI more accessible, adaptable, and widely available." — Open Weights letter signatories

> "To be sure, open weights carry real and distinct risks. Once released, the weights are beyond the original developer's control, and modified versions are difficult to trace or reverse. But the right response to this risk is not to prohibit open weights." — Open Weights letter signatories

> "In fact, openness may be one of the most important paths to AI safety and security. Relying solely on closed models is not inherently safe: they can be breached, misused, or fail in ways that outsiders cannot detect." — Open Weights letter signatories

> "Just as open-source software demonstrated that transparency can be more secure than obscurity, AI safety may depend on giving more people the ability to test and strengthen the models on which society relies." — Open Weights letter signatories
