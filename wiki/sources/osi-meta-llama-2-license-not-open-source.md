## Summary

In a July 20, 2023 opinion post by Stefano Maffulli, the [[OpenSourceInitiative]] (OSI) states that [[Meta]] has created the misunderstanding that LLaMa 2 is "open source" and that the Llama license is not an Open Source license. The OSI argues that the license fails the Open Source Definition (OSD) bar on discriminating against persons, groups, or fields of endeavor. It points to two parts of the license: a commercial-use restriction for some users in paragraph 2 and the purpose restrictions of the Acceptable Use Policy. The OSI says it has asked Meta to correct the statement, and that it is running events to reach a common definition of "open" for AI systems.

## Key Claims

- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that the license for the Llama LLM is not an Open Source license, despite Meta describing LLaMa 2 as "open source"
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says it has asked [[Meta]] to correct its misstatement that LLaMa 2 is open source
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — welcomes Meta lowering barriers to access to powerful AI systems while rejecting the open-source label
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues Meta is confusing "open source" with "resources available to some users under some conditions"
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — questions whether the term "open source" can validly apply to a large language model made of several kinds of resources at all
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states that under OSD points 5 and 6, an Open Source license may not discriminate against persons, groups, or fields of endeavor
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues paragraph 2 of the Llama Community License Agreement breaks the OSD by restricting commercial use for some users
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues the Llama Acceptable Use Policy breaks the OSD by restricting use of the model and software for certain purposes
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — says it does not question Meta's wish to limit competitive use of Llama, but holds that doing so removes the license from the Open Source category
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues an Open Source license gives developers and users sovereignty to decide how and where to use a technology without engaging another party
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — explains that the OSD bars field-of-use restrictions because future uses cannot be known beforehand, citing the Linux kernel's spread into medical devices, airplanes, and rockets
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues Meta's policy prohibits use in potentially beneficial areas such as regulated or controlled substances and critical infrastructure
- [analysis] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — argues that even a clause requiring users to follow the law is problematic when laws differ between places or are unjust
- [fact] [[OpenSourceInitiative]] — states it is running a series of events to craft a common definition of "open" in the AI context and is inviting submissions of ideas

## Key Quotes

> "Meta is confusing 'open source' with 'resources available to some users under some conditions,' two very different things." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], author of the OSI opinion post

> "OSI does not question Meta's desire to limit the use of Llama for competitive purposes, but doing so takes the license out of the category of 'Open Source.'" — [[StefanoMaffulli]], author of the OSI opinion post

> "The license for the Llama LLM is very plainly not an 'Open Source' license. Meta is making some aspect of its large language model available to some, but not to everyone, and not for any purpose." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], author of the OSI opinion post

> "Open Source is premised on the understanding that everyone gets to share no matter who you are." — [[StefanoMaffulli]], author of the OSI opinion post
